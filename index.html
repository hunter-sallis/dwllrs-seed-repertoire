What it sounds like

A loud, clean, close pop record that never gets busy.

Verse: airy and almost bass-free (about 4% of the energy sits below 120 Hz), mid-bright, one sustained chord bed and the voice carrying everything. Chorus: the floor drops in (low end roughly 3.5× the verse), the top end darkens (spectral centre falls from ~1.8 kHz to ~1.3 kHz) and the dynamics flatten. It gets thicker, not faster or louder.

Drums are felt more than heard. The percussive share is about 0.28, in the lowest fifth of the 80 clips measured, and sharp attacks thin out to one every few seconds once the full arrangement is in. The chord loop is a four-chord cycle at 103 BPM, minor-leaning, and the song exhales into a quiet outro instead of ending on a hit.

Words that the numbers support: hazy, smooth, held-back, late-night, sustained, tonal. Words they do not support: jangly, driving, anthemic, drum-forward, acoustic-strummy.

Measured against 71 candidate recordings, the record's nearest neighbours are not the Christian surf-rock bands that share its playlists. They are hazy, pop-finished songs with the drums held back: a prayer over an organ bed, a dream-pop single, two soft folk-pop records, and a decade-old acoustic viral. The faith lane survives, but only its pop-produced end.

Limits

  • Measurements use 30-second Apple previews for candidates and three windows plus the full track for the seed; previews usually cover a chorus, so verse textures are under-represented.
  • The metric scores timbre, brightness, rhythm density, bass weight, dynamics, texture, mode and tempo. It does not hear lyrics, vocal character, melody or mood; those come from attributed descriptions.

  • Shazam blocked requests after about 25 pages, so BPM/label data from Shazam covers only part of the pool; tempos in the measured table are librosa estimates from previews and can land on 3:2 or 2:1 relatives.
